# Beaches and silence

## First rule: stop looking for the most beautiful beach

The wrong question is: "which is the most beautiful beach?" On the La Maddalena archipelago every shoreline has its own hour, its own light, its own wind. The right question is: with this wind, at this hour, where will I find shelter and the clearest water?

## Pink granite: the material of the landscape

The archipelago's granite is not background — it is the protagonist. Rock smoothed by marine erosion forms the boulders you sit on, the passages between coves, the underwater canyons. The colour — warm pink, almost orange at certain sunsets — shifts every hour of the day. Bringing a book and staying two hours on the same rock is not idleness: it is reading the territory.

## Archipelago beaches, by character

Cala Coticcio on Caprera is small, white-sand, postcard water. Reached on foot from Caprera or by dinghy. Arriving at dawn means finding the quiet version — the one residents know and quietly protect.

Cala Corsara on Spargi is wide and lunar in shape, with a water-colour gradient from pale blue to ink. It works best with the Mistral because the facing side is sheltered.

The Pink Beach at Budelli is accessible only by sea, with a strict no-landing rule on the pink sand, protected since 1994. Viewed from the water, from anchor, with a secular ceremony. It is the only beach in the Mediterranean where landscape respect is the written rule, not the implied common sense.

Bassa Trinità on La Maddalena has fine sand, a shallow floor and is good for families. Out of peak season it is almost possible to have it to yourself.

## The right time to go

Before ten. After five. Everything else belongs to the charter boats.
