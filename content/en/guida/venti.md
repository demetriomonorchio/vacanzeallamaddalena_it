# Archipelago winds

## Read the wind before choosing your beach

On the La Maddalena archipelago, wind is not accessory meteorology — it is the architecture of the landscape. Every island has a windward and a leeward side, and learning to tell them apart is worth more than any forecast app. Locals don't check their phones before heading out: they watch the direction of boats in harbour and the cloud profile over Corsica.

## Mistral: master of the western shore

The Mistral blows from the north-northwest and rules the summer. It brings dry air, quartz skies and visibility stretching to Corsica. But it stirs the open sea and makes the north-western beaches unsuitable for anyone seeking calm water.

**Where to go with the Mistral:**

Porto Massimo, on the north-east of La Maddalena island, is sheltered by the promontory and becomes a basin of still, transparent water — the Mistral delivers water clarity without wave action. Cala Spalmatore, on the northern side of the main island, sits in a semi-sheltered position. The water stays luminous, the beach is never crowded, and the morning light on the granite is difficult to forget. On Spargi, Cala Corsara is wide, protected to the south-west by the island's profile: with a strong Mistral, it is one of the few beaches in the archipelago where swimming remains undisturbed.

## Scirocco: the wind of warmth and rest

The Scirocco arrives from the south-east, bringing humidity and heavier light. The sea builds on La Maddalena's southern shore while calming on the northern one. It is the wind that divides tourists from habitués: those who know the archipelago already know where to turn.

**Where to go with the Scirocco:**

Bassa Trinità, on the northern shore of La Maddalena, is placed in the lee of the Scirocco by the island's own geometry — flat water, sandy floor, ideal for families. On Caprera, Cala Coticcio is reachable on foot or by dinghy and sits in a small north-facing bay: with a moderate Scirocco it is among the most sheltered spots in the whole archipelago. Cala Portese, also on the north-eastern side of Caprera, offers shelter from the Scirocco and a blue-green colour with no equal in the Mediterranean.

## Libeccio: the wind to manage

The Libeccio blows from the west-southwest and is the most unpredictable. It builds sea on the north-western shore but opens windows of calm on the eastern side. It is not the wind for beach picnics — it is the wind for reading under a pergola and for the most chromatic sunsets of the week.

## Thermal breeze: the daily rhythm

Every day around eleven in the morning a sea breeze begins to climb inland. At dusk it often reverses into a cooler, softer land breeze. This cycle is the island's metronome: it explains why locals swim at seven in the morning, why fishermen return to port at noon, and why aperitivo is always held outdoors.

## Reading the wind without instruments

Archipelago water stays transparent even in a strong Mistral, as long as you choose the right side. A practical read: if the pennant on moored sailing boats points north-east, the sheltered side is to the east. If it points north-west, look west. It works every time and requires no data connection.
