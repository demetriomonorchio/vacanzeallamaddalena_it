# Budelli and Spargi: the islands worth reaching

## Two islands, two opposite characters

Budelli and Spargi are the most iconic destinations on the daily island tour. Both are reached by boat from La Maddalena in under an hour, and in both cases the experience is radically different from any beach reachable by road. Having a base in La Maddalena — rather than arriving on a day-charter from the mainland — means choosing when to depart, according to wind and hour.

From our apartments in La Maddalena you can organise your day at Budelli or Spargi leaving at seven in the morning, when the water is still clear and the light is lateral.

## Island of Budelli

Budelli is the most famous and the most inaccessible. The island is publicly owned and its Spiaggia Rosa — the only beach in the world with that colouration — has been protected by decree since 1994: landing on the sand and removing any material from the shore are prohibited.

The pink colour is not an optical effect: it comes from microscopic fragments of Miniacina miniacea, a foraminifera that lives on the shallow surrounding seabed. In the decades before protection, intensive tourist sand removal had already visibly reduced the colour. Protection has slowed the damage but not halted it entirely: every year some sand is shifted by winter currents.

Spiaggia Rosa is seen from the water, from anchor, in silence. It is one of the few places in the world where respect for the landscape is the written rule, not the implied hope.

Spiaggia del Cavaliere, on the opposite side of Budelli, is accessible. Less photographed, less visited, with a floor that slopes slowly over pale sand. It works best in the afternoon, when the Mistral eases and the light changes direction.

## Island of Spargi

Spargi is larger and more varied than Budelli, with a jagged profile dividing it into sides with distinct characters.

Cala Corsara is the main beach: wide, with pink granite behind and a water-colour gradient from transparent pale blue to deep blue. With the Mistral it is sheltered by the north-western headland — one of the few beaches in the archipelago where swimming is comfortable even in strong wind. With the Libeccio, wave action enters and the beach loses its calm.

Cala d'Alga, smaller and less accessible, lies on the northern side of Spargi and is almost always empty — few boats can anchor in the shallow water in front of the beach. The water is among the most transparent in the entire archipelago.

The Spargi wreck is one of the most visited dive sites in the Mediterranean: a Roman ship from the second century BC, sunk with its cargo of wine amphorae, visible at less than ten metres depth on the north-western side of the island.

## How to plan the day

Guided island tours depart from La Maddalena each morning, generally between 9 and 10. By booking in advance or arranging directly with the operator, earlier departures are possible — some run dawn outings for those who want the beaches clear.

Alternatively, with a boat hire (licence required for vessels above 40 hp) you can build your own itinerary: Spargi and Budelli in the morning, return to La Maddalena in the late afternoon, after planning the day around the wind direction.

From Isola, Madda or Lena to Cala Gavetta harbour: under five minutes on foot.
