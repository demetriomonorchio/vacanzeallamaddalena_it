# Responsible tourism in the archipelago

## It is not an obligation. It is the reason you are here.

The beauty of the La Maddalena archipelago is not a permanent given. It is the result of decades of protection, respected rules and choices made daily by those who live here and those who visit. How you engage with this territory is not a style question — it is the condition for finding the same clear waters next summer.

Choosing to stay in an apartment in La Maddalena — rather than a resort outside the village or a motorboat charter for the week — is already a lower-impact choice: local spending, movement on foot, a pace shaped by the place rather than a fixed programme.

## What the Park protects

The archipelago holds ecosystems that do not exist elsewhere in the Mediterranean in the same concentration. Posidonia oceanica meadows clean the water and produce oxygen. The rocky seabeds around Caprera and Spargi shelter red gorgonian fans, lobster and grouper. The coastal dunes of some minor beaches are among the last intact dunes in northern Sardinia.

Marine wildlife includes bottlenose dolphins frequenting the Bocche di Bonifacio, fin whales transiting the Pelagos Sanctuary, and sea urchins whose collection — even a single specimen — is prohibited and fined.

## The Code of Good Conduct

The Park authority has formalised a Code of Good Conduct applicable to all: residents, boaters, hikers, divers. The main points concern anchoring (prohibited over Posidonia meadows), noise in rest areas, collection of marine organisms and trampling of coastal dunes.

In practice: do not anchor where the water is bright green (likely Posidonia beneath); collect nothing from the water or rocks; do not cross marked dunes; carry all waste out from the boat or beach.

## Plastic-free archipelago

The "Plastic-free archipelago" project is a local initiative supported by the Park and private operators. It covers separate waste collection across all islands, reduction of single-use plastics in tourism services and voluntary participation in beach clean-up days organised each season.

Our apartments provide reusable water bottles and information on waste separation points in the village. Recycling on the main island functions well: glass, paper, plastic and organic waste have separate collections.

## An apartment as the most consistent choice

An apartment in the village has a fundamentally different impact from a large resort or a motorboat burning fuel to move between stops each day. You shop at the local market, cook with island produce, walk instead of drive. It is not a militant stance — it is simply the way to experience this territory more fully.

Isola, Madda and Lena are designed for this: for those who want the Park, not an image of it.
